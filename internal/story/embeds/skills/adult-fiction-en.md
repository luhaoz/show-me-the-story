# Adult Fiction Craft (Fixed Level 4)

This skill supports explicitly adult fictional scenes. Maximum explicitness does not require an intimate scene in every chapter, and explicit vocabulary must never replace character, causality, or story movement.

## Non-negotiable boundaries

- Every participant must be unambiguously established as an adult. Do not infer age from appearance, setting, or implication.
- Every intimate interaction must be clearly consensual. Treat active agreement, requests, responses, pauses, and withdrawal as meaningful choices. Silence, freezing, intoxication, unconsciousness, coercive authority, or incapacity is not consent.
- If age is unclear or the situation involves a minor, coercion, exploitation, or illegality, do not produce adult description. Continue with non-intimate story action or require corrected premises.
- Never frame ignored refusal as flirtation, a boundary violation as romance, or objectification as characterization.

## Outline and scene design

Before outlining an intimate scene, establish why it happens, what each character wants, what boundaries matter, and what changes afterward. Build escalation from existing attraction, trust, conflict, or choice rather than changing personalities to force a scene.

A useful flow is approach, confirmation, escalation, turn, and aftermath, without labeling those beats in the prose. Each escalation needs observable initiative or response. Address hesitation before continuing. Leave a consequence in emotion, trust, commitment, secrecy, power, or plot.

## Prose and continuity

- Match the established POV, period, genre, and each character's vocabulary. Choose a predominantly direct, literary, or contemporary-commercial register for the scene instead of switching at random.
- Select anatomical language from established character information rather than inferring bodies, sexual roles, or initiative from names, gender identity, or relationship labels.
- Prefer precise verbs and observable responses. Use explicit anatomical terms only when they clarify position, action, or POV; do not repeat them in adjacent sentences merely to maintain intensity.
- Vary pace through approach, pattern, interruption, adjustment, and renewed choice. Avoid a uniform sequence of mechanical actions.
- Select a few sensory details with a clear source: temperature, pressure, texture, weight, breath, fabric, sound, light, or scent. Do not inventory every sense.
- Maintain spatial continuity: relative positions, hands, clothing, furniture, and transitions must remain physically possible.
- Dialogue should confirm boundaries, express wants, reveal character, or alter the relationship. Do not reduce every character to generic commands and praise.
- Nonverbal feedback may supplement but never replace clear consent. Ambiguous responses require a pause and clarification.

## Climax, transition, and aftermath

Earn a climax through accumulating pace, narrowed attention, physical response, and character choice rather than replacing the process with a stock phrase. Do not automatically equate pain, tears, unconsciousness, or incapacity with pleasure.

Transition into and out of intimacy through distance, dialogue, touch, adjustment, or emotional turns. Afterward, include only the care appropriate to these characters—checking in, handling discomfort, cleaning up, hydration, warmth, space, conversation, or quiet presence—and preserve at least one consequence for later chapters.

## Revision checks

1. Does the scene preserve established characterization and relationship history?
2. Is every participant clearly adult and is consent observable throughout escalation?
3. Does each action follow physically from the last one?
4. Are vocabulary, dialogue, and metaphor consistent with POV and genre?
5. Can repeated anatomy terms, adverbs, sound effects, ellipses, or generic reactions be cut?
6. Does the scene change character, relationship, conflict, or plot?
7. Has revision preserved story facts and boundaries rather than escalating behavior without authorization?

Avoid clinical-manual prose, mechanical checklists, anatomically impossible movement, exaggerated universal reactions, mixed metaphors, repetitive sound effects, abrupt vulgarity, or an intimate scene inserted only because this skill is enabled.
